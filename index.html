Chapter 01 · Maestro Studio

Cut spatial stories like a film editor.

On the Mac, Maestro Studio works the way a cutting room does: a source monitor and a live 3D program view side by side, above a full-width, multi-track timeline. Every reveal, move, fade, video and sound in your scene is a clip you can drag, trim and rearrange, frame by frame.

  • Derived clips, not busywork. Clips are a live projection of your actions: reveal to hide, play to stop. Drag a clip and the underlying actions retime with it.
  • Drag-to-add action library. Eighteen categorized action chips, from Show and Fade to Play Video and Spark Burst. Drop one on a track and it snaps to the step boundary.
  • A real source monitor. Open any take in the Source Previewer, mark In and Out with I and O, then drag it to the timeline and the clip lands already trimmed to your selection.
  • Non-destructive by default. Trim changes a clip's length; slip changes which frames it shows. Both are just timing data, and the master file is never touched until you consolidate on purpose.
  • Frame-accurate, snap-aware. Every drag lands on the frame grid, edges magnetize to steps, clips and the playhead, and one glance at the Snap control tells you whether the magnet is on.
  • Copy and paste that gets it. Clips paste at the playhead with their spacing intact. Keyframes paste onto other objects. Transforms copy whole, or just position, rotation or scale.
  • Block before the footage exists. Placeholders stand in for videos and models at real size and length. Animate them, gate them, time them, then drop in the finished file and everything carries over.
  • Compose for a person, not a screen. Immersive guides draw the floor, eye level, comfort zone, distance rings and field of view in the viewer, at seated or standing height.
Chapter 02 · Maestro Vision

Step inside. Sculpt the scene with your hands.

On Vision Pro, your chapter isn't a preview. It's the editor. Look at an entity and pinch to move it. Summon a gizmo ring to rotate on a world axis. Keyframe a pose from where you're standing. The room is the canvas.

  • Gaze-pickable everything. Entities, gizmo handles, timeline clips: look, pinch, drag. Direct manipulation is always on, and a Move, Rotate or Scale gizmo is one tap away.
  • A full studio in one window. Session rail, asset and action libraries, spatial timeline, animation graph dock, and inspectors, floating wherever you want them.
  • Import from where you are. Pull media straight from Photos or Files on the headset. It lands in the scene instantly and uploads to the Mac in the background.
  • Solo mode. No Mac around? Create, open and save full .chapterscript bundles entirely on-device, with undo, redo and recents.
Chapter 03 · Animation

Ten channels. Bézier-native. One truth.

Keyframes live on the sequence: position, rotation and scale on every axis, plus opacity. Real tangent handles, per-key interpolation, and continuous Euler rotation that never flips. The same evaluator drives the Mac, the headset, and playback. What you author is what plays.

  • A real graph editor. Channel chips, draggable keys with axis lock and frame-grid snap, unified or broken tangents, pinch zoom, double-tap to insert. On Mac and Vision Pro alike.
  • Auto-key that respects you. Recording only re-keys channels you've already animated. A fresh channel is never keyed by accident.
  • Motion trails in the room. On Vision Pro, animated entities draw their path through space. Grab an orb on the trail and reshape the move where it happens.
  • Rotation done right. Continuous Euler degrees with explicit rotate order and live filtering. Wind up three full turns if the story calls for it.
Chapter 04 · Live Sync

Save on the Mac. Blink on the headset.

An embedded server publishes your chapter on the local network. Every ⌘S goes straight to the headset, and edits flow the other way too. Move an entity on Vision Pro and it lands in the Mac's undo stack as one clean entry.

Chapter 05 · And the rest

Everything a spatial storyteller actually needs.

Immersive backdrops

Wrap a sequence in a 360° or 180° video sphere (mono, side-by-side, over-under or MV-HEVC) or a full USDZ environment.

Interaction gates

Hold a step until the audience taps, looks at, walks up to, or grabs the thing you choose, or until a timeout passes. With an optional on-screen prompt.

Passthrough or portal

Per-sequence presentation: fully immersive, mixed with the real room visible, or a plain window. Switch mid-story.

Primitives, lights & text

Drop in spheres, boxes, planes, 3D text and lights straight from the add menu. Block out a scene with no external modeling.

Asset folders

Organize scene assets and backdrops into folders, duplicate and re-target with a right-click, and keep big projects navigable.

Deep undo, everywhere

Every timeline drag, inspector tweak and spatial edit is one clean undo entry, on the Mac and on the headset.

Photos & Files import

Local-first media on Vision Pro: picks land in the scene instantly, hash-matched and background-uploaded to the Mac with retry.

Steps that hold curves

Playback gates pause the animation clock, not the story. Curves hold their pose and resume exactly where they left off.

Typed forms + raw JSON

Every action has a typed parameter form, plus a raw JSON escape hatch when you want to hand-tune the long tail.

Track groups

Fold a run of related tracks into one named, collapsible row. A folded group still shows every clip in miniature, and grouping never changes timing.

A library with bins

File media into bins that are labels, never storage. Browse folders on your Mac without leaving the app, and an import sheet asks what each video should become.

Audio you can hear

Auditioning is real: playback is audible, scrubbing plays the moment under the playhead, and volume rides are keyframed per channel. The editor's master volume never ships.

Chapter 06 · Open format

Your story is a readable file, not a black box.

Chapters save as ChapterScript, an open, documented directory bundle: one pretty-printed chapter.json plus your assets, manifested with SHA-256 hashes. Diff it, version it, generate it, play it anywhere the format is implemented.

chapter.json
{
  "formatVersion": 3,
  "id": "the-lighthouse",
  "displayName": "The Lighthouse",
  "defaultSequenceId": "arrival",
  "sequences": [{
    "id": "arrival",
    "presentation": "immersive",
    "immersiveBackdrop": {
      "kind": "video",
      "file": "shore_360.mov",
      "field": "equirect360",
      "loop": true },
    "steps": [{
      "duration": 8,
      "gate": { "type": "tap" },
      "actions": [
        { "kind": "revealEntity",
          "reveal": { "entity": "lantern",
            "fadeIn": 1.5 } } ] }],
    "animationTracks": [ /* bézier keys */ ]
  }]
}
  • One bundle, everything in it. chapter.json plus an assets/ folder. Copy it, zip it, ship it.
  • Sorted-keys, pretty-printed. Saves are deterministic, so version control diffs show exactly what changed in your story.
  • Integrity built in. Every asset is manifested with byte size and SHA-256, so players cache aggressively and never show a stale frame.
  • Made to outlive the tools. Forward-compatible migration on open; tolerant decoding on every field added since v1.

Open source · Powers Maestro

chapterscript

Every chapter you author in Maestro is a .chapterscript bundle: an open, documented JSON format, with ChapterPlayer, the open source visionOS runtime, performing playback. Both are MIT licensed and tool-agnostic. Your stories are never locked in; Maestro is simply the best way to write them.
